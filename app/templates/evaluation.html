Why is Random Forest performing so much better?

1. **Non-Linear Relationships**: Retail demand sales values exhibit non-linear interactions. For example, promotion effects (`onpromotion`) can vary highly depending on the product category (`family`). A linear regression struggles with these multiplicative relationships without complex manual feature interactions.

2. **Categorical Store-Family Dynamics**: With 54 stores and 33 product categories, there are $54 \times 33 = 1,782$ distinct store-family configurations. The Random Forest easily splits these variables and learns localized tree splits for specific stores and product combinations, whereas linear models treat them as additive parameters.

3. **Zero-Sales Boundaries**: Retail sales are naturally bounded at 0 (a store cannot sell a negative number of units). Linear Regression frequently yields negative predictions which we must manually clip, introducing error. Decision tree models naturally restrict predictions to the range of training labels, preventing structural prediction errors.
